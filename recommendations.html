Recommendations

I teach at UNC Charlotte as an adjunct, and I get a fair number of recommendation requests. I am glad to write them. I also have less time than a full-time faculty member does, so I run a process that gets you a better letter faster.

What to do

  1. Write the recommendation yourself, as if you were me , in a shared Google document. You know what you did in my course and what this application wants; I know how to sound like me. Start from your draft and we both save a week.
  2. Paste your resume below that, in the same document.
  3. Paste a bullet list below that of anything else you think I should weigh — projects, context, the thing you are proud of that never showed up in a grade.
  4. Email me a link to the document. Give me edit access, not comment access.

Your draft is raw material, not the finished letter. I revise it, I cut what I cannot honestly say, and I sign only what I believe. Writing it yourself speeds me up; it does not put words in my mouth.

Include all of this

  • Deadlines. Every one of them, with the date the recipient needs the letter in hand rather than the date you would like me to finish.
  • Your GPA.
  • What you are applying for. The specific program, scholarship, job, or transfer — not the category. A letter aimed at a particular thing beats a general one every time.
  • Anything else you think matters. Submission mechanics count here: whether a portal emails me a link, whether someone wants a PDF, whether a form demands a signature.

Then harass me mercilessly

I mean this. As the deadline approaches, remind me — and keep reminding me until you can see the letter has landed. I would much rather field five nudges than miss your deadline, and you should never assume that silence means I have it handled.

Start about two weeks out, and escalate. This is the one context in which I will never find you annoying.

If you keep on doin' what you've been doin' yer gonna keep on gettin' what you've been gettin!
