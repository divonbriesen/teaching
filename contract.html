Contract

Every course site has a contract page. It's short, and you sign it with your name and the date. Use the text below as written and fill in the brackets.

The Text

I, [Your Full Name], agree to abide by the terms of the course contract in my [Term Year] [COURSE CODE], [Course Name] class with my instructor, Professor D.I. von Briesen.

I understand that all work that I do on publicly available online tools will be available to the world and will not put information there that is inappropriate for schoolwork, or that I wish to keep private. I also understand that it is my work that counts for attendance, not logins or showing up for class.

As such, failure to turn in assignments may show as absences. I also understand that given the structure and content of this class it's possible to find many examples online or even view my classmates' code directly. I swear that I will only use these resources to learn and will not cut and paste code except where I have properly given credit and never from my classmates.

Signed:
[Your Full Name], [Date]

What Gets Checked

  • The page is contract.html, it's in your header navigation, and its heading says Contract.
  • It's several paragraphs, the three above, each in its own <p>. Don't squeeze them into one block.
  • Your name in the first line is a link back to your introduction. That's the only link in that paragraph.
  • Your signature is in italics after the last paragraph: Signed: your name, then the date you signed. Use the italic tag (<em>), not a font trick in CSS.
  • Fill in every bracket. Your course code and name are the ones on your schedule, like ITIS 3135 or WEB250.
  • The link goes both ways: your introduction's list of courses has this course linked to your contract. See the Introduction Webpage Standards.

Why There's a Contract

Your work is public, and it's your work that counts, not logging in. Signing says you read that. It also gives your course site one more real page with real content on it.
