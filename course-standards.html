Course Standards

These apply in every course I teach, at the university and at the community college, whether or not the subject has anything to do with the web. They are about how you work, not what you build. The rules for what you build live under Web Standards.

Where these standards and your course syllabus disagree, the syllabus wins — and tell me, because one of them needs fixing.

Canvas is the source of truth

What is due, and when, lives in Canvas. This site explains how to do the work and what "done" means; it does not carry the schedule, because two copies of a schedule means one of them is wrong. If Canvas and a page here disagree about a date, Canvas is right.

Submit what was asked for

  • Turn in the format requested. A link when a link was asked for, a file when a file was asked for.
  • If the assignment asks for a URL, submit the URL of the finished page — not your home page, and not a folder listing.
  • Late work is governed by your course syllabus. Ask before the deadline, not after; the answer is far more often yes.

Name things so the name answers the question

Files, folders, images, and submissions. A grader looking at a list of names should know what each one is without opening it. This is the same habit whether you are naming an image or a variable, and it is the cheapest professional skill you will ever pick up.

Do your own work, and say what helped

  • The work you submit must be yours. Copying another student's work, or submitting something you cannot explain, is not.
  • Tools — including generative AI — are governed by the policy in your course syllabus, which differs by course and by institution. When a tool is permitted, disclose what you used it for. When you are not sure whether it is permitted, ask first.
  • The test I actually apply: can you explain what you turned in, and change it if I ask you to? If yes, you learned something.

Ask early, in public where you can

Most questions are not unique to you. Use the course discussion space so the answer helps everyone, and save email for matters that are genuinely about you alone. A question asked two days before the deadline gets a much better answer than the same question asked two hours before.

Accessibility and accommodations

If you have an approved accommodation, send me the letter through your institution's process as early in the term as you can, and let's talk about how it applies to this course specifically. If something about how this course is run is getting in the way of your learning and you do not have a formal accommodation, tell me anyway.

Back up your work

A lost file is not an extension. Keep your work somewhere that survives a dead laptop — version control, cloud storage, a second copy, anything but one folder on one machine.
