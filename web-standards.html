Web Standards

These apply in every web course I teach — WEB115, WEB215, WEB250, and ITIS3135. They are the letter of the law: each one is checkable, and each one is actually checked. How you work, as opposed to what you build, is covered by Course Standards.

They are machine-checked, not a matter of taste

Every page on this site loads the Vicunadator — the llama in the corner. Right side up means every rule passed; upside down means something failed, and clicking it tells you exactly what. The same script runs on your pages, so you can see your grade forming before you submit.

Add it to any page you build, and link this copy rather than saving your own, because a copy goes stale the moment a standard changes: 

<script src="https://divonbriesen.github.io/teaching-web/tools/standards-check.js" defer></script>

Pages that predate the teaching hub link an older frozen copy at divonbriesen.github.io/web123/scripts/standards-check.js. That URL still works and will keep working, but new work should use the one above.

The rules come in three layers

The checker reports them in this order, and it is worth understanding why a rule applies before you argue with it.

  • General rules apply to every page, in every course, always. Semantic structure, a favicon that resolves, stylesheets in styles/ with default.css first, scripts in a folder rather than loose, at least two fonts, no banned fonts, link colors you chose on purpose, images in images/ with names that say what they are, dividers spaced, and hrefs with no stray whitespace.
  • Site rules depend on what kind of site the page belongs to — a course site, a hobby site, a design firm, a mascot company. The checker works this out from the folder the page is in, so a page inside web115/ is held to course-site rules without you telling it anything.
  • Page rules are specific to the assignment: what an introduction page must contain, what the pictures page must show, which links the navigation must carry by which module.

The authoritative list is not this page — it is standards/rules.json, which is what the checker actually reads. If this page and the checker disagree, the checker is right, and tell me so I can fix the prose.

The rules that catch people out

  • Relative links inside your own site. An absolute URL pointing back into your own webspace is a failure, because your CLT and GitHub copies must both work. The exception is the footer's home and site links, which are deliberately absolute so they point at one fixed home regardless of which copy the reader landed on.
  • Explain the non-semantic bits. Prefer a real element to a div or span, and defend every class, id, and inline style you keep with a comment saying why it exists. The checker counts them and asks.
  • Every page needs a real image. The favicon does not count, no matter how you include it.
  • Spacing around dividers. A | or • or ~ gets a space on each side, everywhere on the page, including in your title.
  • Validate while you build. Both validators, every page, all green — not a cleanup pass the night before it is due.

Course-specific additions

Individual courses add requirements on top of these — the exact navigation, the footer credit line, which pages must exist by which module. For ITIS3135, those live on the course's own Standards page, alongside the Commandments, which are the spirit of the law to these rules' letter.
