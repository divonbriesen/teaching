# "Catching Up" Letter — Source Draft

Not published. Saved as source material for a future student-facing webpage
(see the teaching-web-open-items TODO) that walks a struggling/behind
student through what catching up actually requires, instead of restating
this by hand to each student who asks.

Original letter, sent by D.I. von Briesen to an individual student:

---

I'm sorry you've been having trouble. There are a few things to consider:
- I haven't seen you in class. I don't think I've seen you post any questions
  on the discussion forum. You may recall what I said about skipping 75
  minutes a week - a trivial amount of time considering the cost and effort
  of your education.
- 3% is REALLY low. I'm not in front of the gradebook at the moment but that
  suggests you've done almost nothing - or perhaps you have and we're really
  behind on grading. Have you fixed the introduction? Setup GitHub? Setup
  FileZilla? All the M00 material is fundamental, and everything builds on
  top of it.
- The discussions are really just assignments with peer reviews - you need
  only post yours and reply to two others - which should not be
  unmanageable.
- I don't believe I've ever had a student recover to go from 3% to a
  reasonable score in 25 years of teaching. Maybe once, 20 years ago.
- I have many times been happy to accept late work and waive late penalties
  and will do so now as well, but it rarely matters, since the issues that
  plagued students at the start of class don't usually go away; they
  compound.
- Once the withdrawal date passes, I MUST give you a grade, which inevitably
  becomes an F. I hate that.
- I will accept late work. I'll even waive the late penalty.

What I can't do is make you do the work and what I won't do is waive the
requirements.

Please let me know if I can assist you any further. The biggest challenge
I've found is time - and a class like this takes 3-9 hours a week, and you
do an hour a week, by week six (now) you "owe" the class between 12 and 45
hours - JUST TO BE CAUGHT UP.

You could probably do what needs doing in 45 hours, but it would mean doing
little else, and the work will keep coming.

Best,

D.I. von Briesen

---

## Notes for the future webpage

- Needs to be genericized: drop the specific "3%" / "week six" numbers, or
  parameterize them, since this was written for one student's actual
  standing.
- Core points to keep: hours-per-week math ("owed hours" framing), the
  M00-material-is-foundational point, the discussions-are-just-peer-review
  reassurance, the late-work/late-penalty offer, the withdrawal-date
  deadline, and the "I can't do the work for you" boundary.
- Tone: sympathetic but direct; keep the "25 years of teaching" credibility
  note.
