WEB250 • Database Driven Websites

Catalog description

This course introduces dynamic (database-driven) website development. Topics include the use of basic database CRUD statements (create, read, update and delete) incorporated into web applications, as well as in software architecture principles.

Official description from the Combined Course Library of the North Carolina Community College System. 3 credit hours, 2 class hours and 3 lab hours.

Where the site stops being files and starts being data

You arrive able to build pages. You leave able to build something that remembers. The path runs through PHP on your own machine, then PHP on a real host, then SQL, then a login page, then create, read, update, and delete against a live database — CRUD, which is simple to describe and unforgiving to get wrong.

The reading is The Joy of PHP, and recorded lectures cover the parts that go faster with someone talking over them.

How grading works here

Assignments come in three kinds. Autograded work shows a grade the moment you finish it. Simple-graded work gets a grade when I review it, usually after the due date. Peer-reviewed work carries three scores: your submission and two reviews you write.

Your submission is worth two thirds, and the two reviews split the rest. On a 30-point assignment that is 20 for your work and 5 for each review.

A score of 1 always means the same thing: that piece needs work, and feedback follows telling you what. Fix it and the score follows.

Because the total sums three separate scores, the number tells you exactly what is missing once you know the pattern: 

 1  work submitted, needs updates
 2  still needs updates after a second pass
 5  you did one peer review, but did not submit your own work
10  you did two peer reviews, but did not submit your own work
11  two reviews, plus a submission that needs updates
19  work almost there, no peer reviews
20  work is good, peer reviews still missing
25  good work, one good review
30  everything done
33  everything done, plus a little extra credit

Scale it up for larger assignments. If your total puzzles you, look at the three posts individually — the numbers on each will say which part needs attention.
