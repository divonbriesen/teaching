Course Site Instructions

ITSC1110 • Introduction to Computer Science Principles

This is one assignment, and it builds your course site's first three pages: Home, Introduction, and Contract. Each page has its own part below, and they point to each other. The site goes in a folder in your UNC Charlotte webspace: webpages.charlotte.edu/youruserid/itsc1110/. Your personal home page sits one level above it and links down to it. Two more pages, About and the AI Introduction, come later with the final site.

The Folder

Name it itsc1110 - all lowercase, no dashes, no spaces. The checker looks for exactly that. Inside:

  • index.html - the course home page
  • introduction.html - your introduction
  • contract.html - your course contract
  • components - header.html and footer.html, the shared pieces
  • scripts, styles, and images - the include script, your stylesheet (named default.css), and your pictures, each in its own folder

Your home page links down to the folder with a relative link that ends at the folder, not at a page: itsc1110/. The folder sits inside your public_html folder on the server, and it is the only place your course work goes.

Name the Site

Your course site has a name, and the checker reads it from the h1 in your header:

  • Your name, possessive, then your adjective-animal mascot, then a divider you choose, then ITSC1110 (all capitals, no spaces). The mascot's words start with the same letters as the words in your name, in order - Pat Student's Plucky Stork.
  • Every page's title is that site name, a divider, and the page name (the same words as the page's h2).
  • Any divider works - a vertical bar, a diamond, a bullet - as long as it has a space on both sides and you use the same one everywhere.
  • Your home page, one level up, is about you, not the course. Its title and h1 are just your name, with no mascot and no course code. 
<title>Pat Student's Plucky Stork | ITSC1110 | Home</title>

<header>

  <nav>
    <a href="index.html">Home</a>
    <a href="introduction.html">Introduction</a>

  </nav>
</header>

<main>
  <h2>Home</h2>
</main>

The header goes in components/header.html, as described below. The title and the h2 stay in each page. The menu grows when you add About and the AI Introduction later.

Common Headers and Footers

Professional sites don't copy the same header and footer into every page - they reuse one copy. Update it once and every page changes with it.

  1. Get Paul Browne's HTMLInclude script (HTMLInclude.min.js from GitHub) and put it in your scripts folder.
  2. Create the components folder with header.html and footer.html. Put your header and footer in them - including the header and footer elements themselves. The header holds your site name and a navigation that links to all three pages.
  3. In each page, replace the whole header and footer with the include element from the HTMLInclude instructions, and add the script element at the bottom of the page.

View the source of one of your pages. If you can see a header or footer element in the page file itself, it's in the wrong place - the page should hold only the include.

Your footer says who designed the site and carries your slogan in italics, something like this (use your own company and slogan): 

<footer>
  <p>Designed by <em>Pat Student Enterprises</em>, &copy;2026</p>
  <p><em>Where dreams come to crash on the couch!</em></p>
</footer>

The Three Pages

These are the same three pages every course site in my courses starts with, in this order in your menu.

  • Home (index.html) - welcomes a visitor, says what this site is, and points to your introduction and your contract.
  • Introduction (introduction.html) - your Shared Introduction from the course Google Doc, moved onto your own page. It follows the Introduction Webpage Standards, minus the links line.
  • Contract (contract.html) - the course contract, from the shared Contract page: several paragraphs, the brackets filled in, and your signature in italics with the date.

How the Pages Refer to Each Other

The three pages are one piece of work, so each one points to the others:

  • Every page's menu links to all three pages.
  • Your Home page has a sentence in main that links to your introduction and your contract.
  • On your Introduction, in the list of courses you're taking, the item for this course links to your contract.
  • On your Contract, your name in the first paragraph links back to your introduction.

What We Check

  • The folder is named itsc1110, and holds index.html, introduction.html, and contract.html.
  • The h1 reads your name, your mascot, a divider, and ITSC1110, and each title is that name, a divider, and the page name.
  • components/header.html and components/footer.html exist and contain the header and footer elements, and your page source holds only the include.
  • The pages refer to each other. The Vicunadator checks the menu, the contract link in your introduction, and the introduction link in your contract. We read the sentence on your home page ourselves.
  • Every page passes Accumulus and the Vicunadator, and the Web Standards apply. The Vicunadator may say the About link isn't needed yet. That one comes with the final site.

Submitting

With the address, write three things that gave you trouble while you built it and what you learned from each.

Later: About and the AI Introduction

The final site adds two more pages to the same folder, and the menu links to them too.

  • About (about.html) - in a couple of paragraphs and bullets: what this site shows (ITSC1110, with a link to my teaching site), the tools the course uses (VS Code, Emmet, Git and GitHub, the Accumulus validator, and the Vicunadator), and the sites you build - your personal home page, this course site, and your design firm.
  • AI Introduction (ai_introduction.html) - the one difference from the other courses. It's an introduction written by an AI tool instead of by you, described next.

The AI Introduction

Give an AI tool the facts about you, ask it to write your introduction, and publish the result - then be honest about how it did. The page has three parts, in this order:

  1. The AI's introduction, exactly as it wrote it. Don't edit it. Use UNC Charlotte's data-protected Gemini or Copilot, and give it only what you'd be comfortable putting on a public webpage - this page is public.
  2. Your comparison, a short paragraph or two beside your own introduction: what it got right, what it got wrong or invented, what it missed, and what you would never say that way.
  3. Your disclosure: the tool you used, the prompt you gave it, and the acknowledgment statement from the AI Policy.

That one we read ourselves - no checker can tell an honest comparison from a hurried one.
