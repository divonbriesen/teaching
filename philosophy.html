Philosophy

These are the beliefs behind how my courses run. The Course Standards and Web Standards are what those beliefs look like once they turn into rules.

A standard you cannot check is not a standard

If I cannot tell you exactly what "good" means before you start, I have no business grading you against it afterward. So I write the rules for the work down, and a script checks them. You can run that script on your own page as many times as you like, watch it go green, and submit knowing what I will see. Grading should hold no surprises.

The habits outlast the syntax

Whichever framework dominates the job listings when you graduate, it will not be the one that dominated when you enrolled. What survives is smaller and duller: naming a thing so the name answers the question, leaving a comment that explains the decision rather than the line, validating while you build. I teach the fashionable thing because you need it, and I grade the durable thing because you will keep it.

Struggle is the mechanism, not the obstacle

The moment where the page will not center and you cannot see why is not an interruption of learning — it is the learning. My job is to keep that struggle inside the range where you can win it, not to remove it. So I answer the question behind your question, and I would rather hand you the search term than the snippet.

Tools are welcome; understanding is the price

I have no interest in policing which tools you open, and generative AI is not going back in the box. The test I actually apply has not changed in twenty years: can you explain what you turned in, and change it when I ask you to? If yes, the tool made you faster. If no, it did the course for you and you paid tuition to watch.

Everyone starts somewhere, and nobody starts in the same place

In one week I teach students meeting a file system for the first time and students deploying applications. Neither group is the "real" one. The beginner is not behind, and the experienced student is not done — they are working on different edges of the same craft, and both deserve a course that meets them at that edge.
