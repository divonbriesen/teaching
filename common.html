Common Content

Some things are the same in every course I teach: the contract, the policies, the standards, the introductions, the ongoing boards. They are here, once. Canvas, Brightspace, and each course's own pages link to these pages instead of pasting a copy, so when one changes, it changes everywhere.

Agreements and Policies

  • Contract - the course contract every course site carries, with the text to use and how to sign it.
  • AI Policy - where AI tools help your learning, where they get in the way, and how to disclose what you used.
  • Grading - how work turns into a score, and what counts as done.
  • Schedule - the week numbers behind the module numbers, with the breaks marked.

Standards

  • Standards - the overview of every layer of what "good" means in my courses.
  • Course Standards - how you work in any course of mine, whatever the subject.
  • Web Standards - what every page you build must satisfy, including the course site standards.
  • Validation - the Vicunadator and Accumulus: what they check and how to use them.
  • Design Reference - the CRAP principles, the elements of design, and color theory.

Introductions

  • Introductions - the entry every course opens with, and how it is graded.
  • Annotated Introduction - one complete entry with every formatting rule labeled.
  • Sample Introductions - four finished entries to model yours on.
  • Introduction Form - build a form that collects an introduction and rebuilds the page.

Participation

  • Discussion Boards - the ongoing boards and which posts belong on each.
  • Journals and Work Logs - the format for each and what counts as a post.
  • Bonus Participation - points for things that help the class.
  • Peer Review Guidelines - what makes a review useful, with real examples.

Help and About

  • FAQ - the questions students actually ask.
  • Resources - tools and references for every web-development course.
  • About DI, My Name, and Want a Recommendation?

Each course keeps its own pages for what is different about it: its assignments, its units, and its course site instructions. If a course page and a page here ever disagree, this one wins - and tell me, because one of them needs fixing.
