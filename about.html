About DI

I teach computing and web development on both sides of North Carolina's higher education system — at UNC Charlotte, part of the University of North Carolina System, and at a North Carolina community college. The courses I teach run from first contact with a computer through database-driven web applications, which means I spend my days with students who have never written a line of code and students shipping working applications, often in the same week.

How I teach

Two convictions shape every course on this site. The first is that a standard you cannot check is a standard you cannot teach — which is why the rules for the work live in Web Standards, and a script students run themselves checks those rules, before they submit, as many times as they like. Nobody should discover what I wanted after the grade arrives.

The second is that the habits transfer further than the syntax. Naming a file so its name answers the question, explaining a decision in a comment, validating while you build rather than the night before — those outlast whichever framework is fashionable this year. The Course Standards are mostly about those habits.

How to reach me

Ask in the course discussion board when the question could help somebody else, which is most of the time. Use the messaging tool in the course's learning management system when the matter is genuinely about you alone — grades, accommodations, personal circumstances.

If I have not answered and you need an answer, text me. Say who you are, which class you are in, and what you need — I cannot act on a message from an unknown number that just says "hey".

My name

Two words, lowercase v, and both of them together make the surname. If you want the longer version — what the von is doing there, what forms and databases do to it, and why my colleague Manuel Pérez-Quiñones and I keep having the same argument with software from opposite directions — I wrote it up on a page about my name.

Why a vicuña

Every site I build carries a Camelid Software mascot, and the students name their own the same way — which is the point of the naming rule in the standards. Mine is a vicuña: a small, unfussy South American camelid that produces one of the finest fibers in the world and does it without much fanfare. It seemed like the right animal for the job.
