Q: Is This Class Online or in Person?

A: Both. Self Service lists it as hybrid F2F & Online (Async), which means the three course hours are split: we meet in person for 75 minutes a week, and the rest of the work is online and asynchronous. The word "async" on your schedule describes that online portion — it does not mean the class never meets.

Q: Do I Have to Come to the Zoom Session?

A: The in-person meeting is the class. I run Zoom alongside it for two reasons: it records the session so you can review it or catch up if you had to miss, and it lets students in the room put their own screen on the projector to demo what they're building.

If you miss a week, watch the recording before you ask what you missed.

Q: Where Do I Start?

A: Open the module with the current dates on it and do everything in it. That's the whole answer — the module is the checklist.

The first module is genuinely the heaviest one, because getting started means creating accounts, installing tools, and connecting them to each other. It thins out considerably after that. Make a list of what's in the module, check items off as you go, and ask about specific ones on the discussion board when you get stuck.

Q: What's Due, and When?

A: Everything in a module is due at the end of that module's week, and each module carries its dates at the top. Those dates also feed your Canvas calendar and To Do list.

The exceptions are the ongoing boards — journals and work logs — which run all term instead of belonging to one week. See Journal Instructions and Work Log Instructions.

Q: Something Looks Due Before Class Started. Is That Right?

A: No, and if you see it, tell me. Nothing is due before the end of the first week.

Dates get rebuilt every term, and the course moves between 8-week and 16-week formats, so they can't simply be shifted forward — which is exactly how stale dates survive. Report the specific module and item name on the discussion board and I'll fix it. That's a real contribution to the course, and it earns extra credit.

Q: How Do I Keep Track of Everything?

A: Work from the current module rather than from the assignment list. The module shows you what belongs to this week, in order; the full list shows you the whole term at once, which is where the panic comes from.

Remember that journals and work logs won't line up with any single week — post a work log whenever you do the work, not when a date tells you to.

Q: My Gradebook Says Missing, or Gave Me a 0. Why?

A: Canvas flags anything past its due date as missing on its own, and that flag is not a grade I entered. If the due date was wrong, the missing label is wrong too, and it clears when the date is fixed.

If you think a score is actually wrong, post on the grading and regrading board rather than emailing — see Discussion Board Instructions for which board is which.

Q: I Enrolled Late. Do the Earlier Assignments Count Against Me?

A: No. You are not penalized for work that came due before you were in the class. Start with the current module, and tell me on the discussion board that you added late so I know to look for anything that needs adjusting.

Q: What Exactly Do I Do on Codecademy and freeCodeCamp?

A: For the setup assignment, you're getting started and proving it — not finishing a certification.

  • Codecademy: complete the "Welcome to Codecademy" item, so it appears on your profile.
  • freeCodeCamp: begin the Responsive Web Design certification and earn at least 10 points.
  • Both: make your profile public, or I can't see any of it. That's in each site's profile or account settings.

Full details are on the Codecademy & freeCodeCamp instructions.

Q: My Name Isn't in the Introductions Table of Contents.

A: It isn't supposed to be, and this trips up nearly everyone. Do not edit the table of contents. It is a navigation aid, not a sign-up sheet, and adding yourself to it does not create your entry.

Use it the other way around: read down the list to find where your name falls alphabetically, click the name that would come just after yours to jump to that spot in the document, and build your entry there. The table of contents then picks you up on its own.

The full steps and formatting are on the Introductions Instructions, with worked samples in the Intro Samples and every rule labeled in place in the Annotated Intro.

Q: FileZilla Connects but Won't Show My Files.

A: You're almost certainly off campus without the VPN. Your university webspace is a campus service, so from anywhere else you need to connect through Cisco Secure Client at vpn.charlotte.edu first, then reconnect in FileZilla.

Once you're in, your pages belong in public_html — that's the folder the web actually serves. Anything outside it won't appear at your web address.

The VPN is only needed for the university webspace. GitHub Pages and this site are on the open web and need nothing special.

Q: What Goes in a Work Log Post?

A: Your first post of the term is an h3. Every post after that is an h4: copy the h3 heading and append the week and a letter — Week 1A, Week 1B, and so on.

Under each heading, two bullets:

  • day, date, start – end = total, and location
  • what you did — with sub-bullets, one per thing, not a paragraph

Log every 15+ minutes of work as you do it. Full details and a sample are on the Work Log Instructions.

Q: A Link on Your Site Is Broken. Now What?

A: Tell me on the discussion board and you'll earn extra credit for it. Broken links are usually mine to fix, and I often can't see them from where I'm sitting.

Two things to try first. Hard refresh the page (Ctrl+Shift+R, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac), since your browser may be holding an old copy. And if the address contains web123 or teaching-web, it's from an older version of this site — the current home is divonbriesen.github.io/teaching. Old addresses should forward on their own, so one that doesn't is worth reporting.

Q: Can I Ask Something Anonymously?

A: Yes. There's an anonymous board where your name is hidden from me and from everyone else, for questions you'd rather not attach your name to. It's ungraded, and it's there to be used.

Because it's anonymous, I can't reply to you individually — so if you want a direct answer to something specific, one of the named boards will serve you better. See Discussion Board Instructions.
